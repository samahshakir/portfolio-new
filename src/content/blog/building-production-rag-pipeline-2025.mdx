Every RAG tutorial online follows the same script: chunk your documents, embed them, throw them into a vector database, and call it a day. Then you deploy it and your users start getting hallucinated answers stitched together from irrelevant chunks.

I've built RAG systems that serve thousands of queries daily. This is what actually works in production - and what the tutorials leave out.

## Why Naive RAG Falls Apart

The basic retrieve-then-generate pattern breaks down fast:

- **Semantic search misses keyword-critical queries** - "What's the refund policy for order #4821?" needs exact matching, not vibes
- **Fixed-size chunking splits context** - Important information gets cut across chunk boundaries
- **No quality gate** - The LLM generates confident answers from irrelevant retrieved context
- **Single-shot retrieval** - Complex questions need multiple retrieval steps

## Architecture: What a Production RAG Pipeline Looks Like

```
User Query → Query Analysis → Hybrid Search (Dense + Sparse)
                                      ↓
                               Reranking (Cross-encoder)
                                      ↓
                               Context Assembly
                                      ↓
                               Generation + Citation
                                      ↓
                               Faithfulness Check
                                      ↓
                               Response to User
```

Let's break down each stage.

## Stage 1: Smart Chunking

Fixed token-count chunking is the default, and it's the wrong default. Context gets split mid-paragraph, mid-sentence, sometimes mid-word.

### Semantic Chunking

Instead of splitting every 512 tokens, split by meaning. Compute embeddings for each sentence, then split when the cosine similarity between consecutive sentences drops below a threshold. This keeps coherent ideas together.

### Parent-Child Chunks

Embed small chunks for precision retrieval, but retrieve the parent (larger surrounding context) for generation. This gives you the best of both worlds: accurate matching with sufficient context.

### Contextual Chunking

Prepend each chunk with LLM-generated context: "This chunk is from Chapter 3 of the employee handbook, discussing remote work policies." This dramatically improves retrieval accuracy for ambiguous queries.

**My approach:** Semantic chunking with parent-child hierarchy. Small chunks (200 tokens) for retrieval, parent chunks (800 tokens) for generation context.

## Stage 2: Hybrid Search

Vector search alone isn't enough. It's great for semantic similarity but terrible for exact matches - order numbers, product codes, specific names.

### Dense + Sparse Search

Combine dense vector search (semantic understanding) with BM25 sparse search (keyword matching). Most vector databases now support this natively - Weaviate, Qdrant, and Pinecone all offer hybrid search.

The trick is weighting. I use a 70/30 split favoring dense search, but bump sparse weight to 60% for queries that contain identifiers, numbers, or quoted strings. A simple regex check on the query handles routing.

**Result:** Retrieval recall improved from 72% to 89% after adding BM25.

## Stage 3: Reranking

Bi-encoder embeddings are fast but shallow. They compress entire passages into single vectors, losing nuance. A cross-encoder reranker reads the query and each candidate passage together, producing a much more accurate relevance score.

### Two-Stage Retrieval

1. **First pass:** Hybrid search retrieves top 20 candidates (fast, broad)
2. **Second pass:** Cross-encoder reranker scores and reorders to top 5 (slow, precise)

I use Cohere Rerank in production for its speed-accuracy balance. For self-hosted setups, BGE-reranker-v2 or ColBERT are solid open-source alternatives.

**Result:** Answer relevancy jumped from 0.74 to 0.91 after adding reranking.

## Stage 4: Agentic RAG

Some questions can't be answered in a single retrieval step. "Compare our Q1 and Q2 revenue and explain the difference" requires at least two separate retrievals plus reasoning.

### Multi-Step Retrieval

Instead of one-shot retrieve-and-generate, let the LLM decide:

1. **Analyze the query** - Does it need single or multi-step retrieval?
2. **Plan retrieval** - What specific queries should be issued?
3. **Execute and evaluate** - Are the results sufficient, or do we need another round?
4. **Synthesize** - Generate the final answer from all gathered context

Frameworks like LangGraph make this pattern straightforward. The key insight: let the LLM be the orchestrator, not just the generator.

### Query Decomposition

For complex queries, decompose into sub-queries first. "What changed in our remote work policy after the 2024 review?" becomes:

1. Retrieve current remote work policy
2. Retrieve 2024 review decisions
3. Compare and synthesize

**Result:** Complex query accuracy improved from 54% to 82%.

## Stage 5: Graph RAG

For domains with rich entity relationships - legal documents, medical records, organizational data - flat vector search misses connections between concepts.

### Knowledge Graph + Vector Search

Build a knowledge graph from your documents where entities are nodes and relationships are edges. At query time, retrieve relevant nodes via vector search, then traverse the graph to pull in related context.

Microsoft's GraphRAG approach takes this further: cluster communities of related entities and generate summaries at different granularity levels. This enables both specific and broad questions over the same corpus.

**When to use it:** When your users ask questions that require connecting information across multiple documents. "Which team members worked on projects with Client X?" needs relationship traversal, not just semantic similarity.

## Stage 6: Evaluation

You can't improve what you can't measure. RAG evaluation is harder than standard ML metrics because you're evaluating retrieval and generation jointly.

### Metrics That Matter

- **Faithfulness** - Does the answer only use information from retrieved context? (Catches hallucinations)
- **Answer Relevancy** - Does the answer actually address the question?
- **Context Precision** - Are the retrieved chunks actually relevant?
- **Context Recall** - Did retrieval find all the relevant information?

### Automated Evaluation

Use RAGAS or DeepEval to run automated evaluation suites. Build a golden dataset of 50-100 question-answer pairs manually, then track metrics on every pipeline change.

Set up alerts: if faithfulness drops below 0.85, something broke. If context recall drops, your chunking or retrieval changed.

**My evaluation loop:** Weekly automated runs against the golden dataset, plus sampling 5% of production queries for human review.

## Production Lessons

### What I Got Wrong

1. **Over-chunking** - Smaller chunks aren't always better. I started at 128 tokens and missed context constantly. 200-300 tokens is the sweet spot for most domains.
2. **Ignoring metadata** - Filtering by document date, source, and category before vector search eliminates noise that no amount of reranking can fix.
3. **No fallback** - When retrieval confidence is low, say "I don't know" instead of hallucinating. A simple relevance threshold on reranker scores handles this.

### What Worked Immediately

1. **Hybrid search** - Single biggest improvement for the least effort
2. **Reranking** - Second biggest improvement, drop-in addition
3. **Contextual chunk headers** - Cheap to implement, noticeable quality boost

## The Stack

My current production RAG stack:

- **Embeddings:** OpenAI text-embedding-3-large (or BGE-large for self-hosted)
- **Vector DB:** Qdrant (hybrid search, filtering, good performance)
- **Reranker:** Cohere Rerank v3
- **Orchestration:** LangGraph for agentic flows
- **Evaluation:** RAGAS + custom golden dataset
- **Monitoring:** LangSmith for tracing every retrieval and generation step

## Key Takeaways

1. **Naive RAG is a prototype, not a product** - Plan for hybrid search and reranking from day one
2. **Chunking strategy matters more than embedding model** - Spend time here
3. **Evaluate continuously** - Faithfulness and relevancy metrics catch regressions early
4. **Let the LLM orchestrate** - Agentic RAG handles the complex queries that basic RAG can't
5. **Start simple, add complexity when metrics justify it** - Don't build GraphRAG until vector search isn't enough

---

RAG is deceptively simple to demo and genuinely hard to productionize. The gap between "works on 5 test questions" and "works on 5,000 real queries daily" is where most of the engineering lives. Build the evaluation pipeline first, then iterate on everything else with confidence.
